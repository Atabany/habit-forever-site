I make Habit Forever, and I get asked about subscriptions more than anything else. That is fair: a habit tracker is something you hope to use for years, and a monthly charge for a checklist can feel wrong. So here are the honest options, starting with the ones that cost nothing.

Free options already on your iPhone

  • Reminders. A repeating reminder that you tick off each day is the closest thing iPhone has to a built-in habit tracker. It costs nothing and syncs through iCloud if you use it. It has no streaks, no counts and no history you can read at a glance. My guide on how to track habits on iPhone walks through the steps.
  • Notes. A checklist or a small table with dates down the side. Flexible and private, but nothing reminds you and nothing adds up.
  • Paper. A grid on the fridge is hard to ignore. Our printable habit tracker gives you a ready-made monthly sheet for free.

If you only want to take a vitamin every morning, any of these is enough. You do not need to pay anyone.

What Habit Forever gives you for free

The free version is not a trial. There is no countdown and no account to create. You get up to three habits with the core of the app:

  • Check-off habits, or number habits such as pages, glasses, minutes or spending.
  • Goals: reach a number, or stay under a limit, each day or each week, on every day or chosen days.
  • Reminders per habit, which stay quiet on days you already checked in.
  • Notes on check-ins, current and best streaks, and a colorful day-by-day grid for each habit.

Habit records and notes are stored on your device. There is no iCloud sync.

The lifetime option

Habit Forever Pro adds unlimited habits, deeper insights (your strongest days, your usual time of day, month by month), widgets for every habit, and backup export and import. You can buy it yearly, monthly, or once as a lifetime purchase. Lifetime means you pay one time and Pro stays on your Apple Account, with no subscription and nothing to renew. If you get a new iPhone, you restore the purchase with the same Apple Account.

Purchases are handled by Apple, so a yearly or monthly plan is cancelled the usual way in your iPhone's Settings, under your name and then Subscriptions.

Is three habits enough?

Some people tell me three free habits is too few, and for someone tracking ten things it is. But for most people starting out, three is a good number to begin with rather than a cage. In my experience, starting with a dozen habits on day one is a fast way to stop tracking any of them. Here is how I would pick the three:

  1. One habit that matters most. The change you would be proudest of in six months: reading, exercise, sleep.
  2. One easy win. Something small you will almost always do, like taking a vitamin or stepping outside after breakfast. Seeing a streak build keeps you opening the app.
  3. One thing to do less of. Screen time, coffee or daily spending, with a limit instead of a target. See how to track a habit you want to do less.

Make each one specific, such as "read 10 pages after dinner" rather than "read more". If a habit needs to happen a few times a week rather than daily, one habit with a weekly goal covers it; see weekly habit goals.

When a habit feels automatic, you can archive it. Its history is kept, and on the free version it no longer counts toward your three, so a new habit can take its place. Bringing an archived habit back onto the Today screen is a Pro feature, so archive the ones you are truly done tracking.

Which option should you choose?

  • One or two simple daily habits: Reminders or paper. Free, no app needed.
  • Up to three habits with streaks and number goals: Habit Forever's free version, with no time limit.
  • More than three habits, no subscription: an app with a one-time purchase, such as Habit Forever's lifetime option, or Streaks if you want an Apple Watch app.
  • Several devices, including Android or the web: an app with account sync, such as Habitify. See my Habitify comparison.
