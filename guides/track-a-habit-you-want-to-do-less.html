How to track a habit you want to do less

To cut back on a habit, track a limit instead of a target: "at most 2 cups of coffee a day" or "under 2 hours of screen time", where every day you stay under counts as a win. Pair the limit with an if-then plan for the moment the old habit usually starts, and name what you will do instead.

Most habit trackers are built for doing more: more water, more steps, more pages. But a lot of what people want to change is about doing less. Coffee, scrolling, takeaway, impulse spending. A checklist with a tick for "did it" is the wrong shape for that, and I built limits into Habit Forever for exactly this reason. This guide covers the built-in tools first, then what the research says about breaking a habit, then how to track it.

A note before we start: this is about everyday habits. If you are trying to stop drinking, smoking, gambling or using a drug, or a habit feels out of your control, please talk to a doctor or a support service. A tracker can sit alongside that help, but it is not treatment. In the UK, the NHS addiction support page lists where to start.

Built-in tools on iPhone

Screen time is the one habit iPhone measures for you. Screen Time in Settings shows your daily average and how often you pick up your phone, and Time Allowances (App Limits on older iOS versions) let you set a daily budget for app categories such as social media or games. If screen time is your target, set that up first: it measures automatically, which no tracker can match.

For most other habits, coffee, sweets, spending, there is nothing built in that counts them. A tally in the Notes app or on paper works: one mark per cup, and a line under the day when you stayed under your limit. Our printable habit tracker gives you a monthly grid for that, and the habit cost calculator shows what a habit costs you in a year.

What research says about breaking a habit

Habits are triggered by situations: the same time, place, mood or preceding action. That is why willpower alone tends to lose. Two ideas from the research are worth using.

If-then plans. The psychologist Peter Gollwitzer called them implementation intentions: a plan in the form "if situation Y happens, then I will do Z". His 1999 paper in American Psychologist described how linking a specific situation to a specific response helps people act on their goals, and a 2006 meta-analysis with Paschal Sheeran found a positive effect across 94 independent tests. A 2024 review of 642 tests by Sheeran, Listrom and Gollwitzer found the effects were larger when plans used the if-then format and when people were highly motivated to reach the goal. It is a helpful tool, not a guarantee.

Name a replacement. In three experiments, Marieke Adriaanse, Gollwitzer and colleagues (2011) studied if-then plans that replace a habitual response with an alternative one in a critical situation, such as a snack habit. The plans removed the old habit's head start in the mind, but people only changed what they did when they also strongly wanted the alternative. In practice: pick a replacement you actually like.

Put together, a good plan sounds like: "If I want a third coffee after lunch, then I will make a herbal tea." Or: "If I pick up my phone in bed, then I will put it on the charger across the room."

Set a limit, not zero (usually)

Going from four coffees to none is hard, and one slip can feel like total failure. Going from four to two is a goal you can hit tomorrow. A limit also keeps the habit honest: you still see exactly how much you had, so the trend is visible even on a day you went over.

  • Daily limits suit habits that happen most days: coffee, screen time, sweets, daily spending.
  • Weekly limits suit habits that are fine now and then: takeaway meals, nights out, hours of gaming. A weekly limit leaves you free to choose the day.
  • A clean break suits a habit you want gone completely. Track the days without it rather than the slips.

How to track a limit in Habit Forever

  1. Add a new habit, for example "Coffee".
  2. Under Goal, choose Stay under.
  3. Set the number and unit, such as at most 2 cups, and choose Each day or Each week.
  4. Turn on One tap adds with 1, so each cup is a single tap on the habit's circle.

The new habit list has a "Do less of" group with ready-made ideas: screen time under 2 hours a day, coffee under 2 cups, daily spending under a limit in your currency, and "No sweets", a check-off habit for a day without.

Here is how a limit counts:

  • Entries add up. Log each cup or each purchase as it happens; the day's total is what counts.
  • A day with nothing logged counts as zero, so it is a success. You do not have to open the app to record "none today".
  • Going over shows as over, and that day breaks the streak. Your streak is the run of days (or weeks) you stayed at or under the limit.
  • The history stays honest. The day-by-day grid fills in the days you stayed under, and the number you logged is kept, so you can see a week of 3 cups become a week of 2.

For a clean break, either set a limit of 0, so every day with nothing logged counts, or use a check-off habit that you tick for each day without. You can add a note to any check-in, which is a good place for what triggered a slip and what you will try next time.

Reminders help here in a different way: set one for the time the urge usually hits, such as 3 pm for the afternoon coffee, as a prompt to use your if-then plan. Pro adds patterns such as your strongest days of the week, which can show when a limit is easiest and hardest to keep.

After a slip

A day over the limit is information, not a verdict. Look at what happened (the time, the place, how you felt), adjust the if-then plan, and keep the limit for tomorrow. If you keep going over by a lot, the limit may be too ambitious for now: raise it a little, hold it for a few weeks, then lower it again. Read what to do when you break a streak for more on recovering from a bad day.

Cutting back also works best with something new in its place. If you want to stack a good habit onto an existing moment, see habit stacking.

FAQ

How do I track a habit I want to stop?

Track a limit instead of a target, such as at most 2 cups of coffee a day, or for a clean break, track the days without it rather than the slips.

Do I have to log days when I did not do the habit?

No, in Habit Forever a day with nothing logged counts as zero, so it counts as a success for a limit.

What is an if-then plan?

An if-then plan, which researchers call an implementation intention, links a specific situation to a specific response, such as "if I want a third coffee after lunch, then I will make a herbal tea".

Can a habit tracker help with addiction?

A tracker is not treatment; if a habit involves alcohol, smoking, gambling or drugs, or feels out of your control, talk to a doctor or a support service.
