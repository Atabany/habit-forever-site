The milestones, from the research

These figures come from two studies. Lally and colleagues (2010) followed 96 volunteers who each chose a new daily eating, drinking or exercise habit in the same context, such as after breakfast. Singh and colleagues (2024) pooled 20 studies with 2,601 participants. “Automatic” here means the behavior feels like something you do without thinking; it doesn’t mean the habit is finished.

How to read your result

The median is the middle person, not a deadline. Half of the people in Lally’s study took longer than 66 days, and the time varied widely between people and between behaviors, from a glass of water to a daily run. If you’re past day 66 and it still takes effort, you’re not behind. You’re in the normal range.

Missing a day is normal too. In the same study, missing one opportunity did not materially affect how the habit formed. What mattered was repeating the behavior in a consistent context. That’s why a grid of days is more useful than a streak counter alone: it shows the pattern, not just the last break. More on that in what to do when you miss a day.

FAQ

Is it 21 days or 66 days to form a habit?

The best-known measurement found a median of 66 days to reach near-peak automaticity, with a range of 18 to 254 days. The 21-day figure is a popular rule of thumb rather than a measured result for forming habits.

Does the calculator store my data?

No. The calculation runs in your browser and nothing is sent to a server or saved.

Does missing a day reset the clock?

No. In Lally and colleagues’ 2010 study, missing one opportunity did not materially affect the habit formation process. Keep going the next day.

Why is the range so wide?

People, habits and situations differ. Participants chose very different behaviors, from drinking water to exercising, and some never reached a clear plateau during the 84 days they were tracked.
