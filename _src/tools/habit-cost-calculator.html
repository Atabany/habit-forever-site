How it works

  • Times a year = how many times × 365 for a day, × 365 ÷ 7 for a week, × 12 for a month, or × 1 for a year.
  • Cost a year = cost each time × times a year. A week is that ÷ (365 ÷ 7), a month ÷ 12, and 10 years × 10.
  • Time a year = minutes each time × times a year ÷ 60, in hours; ÷ 24 for full days, ÷ 16 for waking days.
  • Cutting back by X% saves X% of each figure, because the cost scales with how often you do it.

It’s simple arithmetic on purpose. It ignores price rises, and it doesn’t add interest you might earn by saving the money instead, so long-run totals are, if anything, on the low side.

Cutting back without quitting

You don’t have to quit to save. A habit you do half as often costs half as much, and a limit you can keep, such as “two coffees a day” or “no more than 30 minutes of social media”, often lasts longer than an all-or-nothing rule. Pick a number a little below what you do now, count it every day, and lower it once it feels easy. My guide on tracking a habit you want to do less goes into how. If you slip one day, carry on the next; a single miss doesn’t undo the habit, as I explain in what a broken streak really means.

FAQ

How much does a coffee a day cost a year?

Multiply the price by 365. At 5 a cup in your currency, one coffee a day is 1,825 a year and 18,250 over ten years. Two a day doubles it.

How much time does an hour of scrolling a day add up to?

365 hours a year, which is about 15 full days or nearly 23 waking days of 16 hours.

Does the calculator include inflation or interest?

No. It multiplies today’s cost by how often you pay it, so real long-run totals are usually higher.

Is my data saved?

No. The numbers stay in your browser; nothing is uploaded or stored.
