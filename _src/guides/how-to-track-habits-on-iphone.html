The simplest way to track a habit on iPhone is a repeating reminder in the Reminders app: you tick it off each day and it comes back tomorrow. That works for one or two habits. Once you want to see a streak, count glasses or pages, or look back over a month, a dedicated habit tracker is the better tool.

Below are four ways to do it, starting with what is already on your phone, with the honest limits of each.

1. The Reminders app (repeating reminders)

Reminders comes with every iPhone and is the closest thing to a built-in habit tracker.

  1. Open Reminders and create a list, for example "Habits".
  2. Add a reminder such as "Read 10 pages".
  3. Open the reminder's details, give it a date and time, and choose a repeat option such as daily, or custom days.
  4. Tick it off when you have done it. The next one appears for the following day.

What it does well: it is free, it syncs through iCloud if you use it, and the notification arrives at the time you choose. Reminders also has widgets, so you can tick items off from the Home Screen.

Where it falls short: a repeating reminder has no memory you can see. There is no streak, no calendar of the days you did it, and no way to say "I drank 6 of 8 glasses". If you miss a day, the old reminder simply stays overdue. Apple notes that future instances of a repeating reminder can only be completed after the current one is marked complete, which makes catching up awkward.

2. The Calendar app

Some people create a repeating all-day event called "Workout" and rename or recolor it on the days they actually did it. Others keep a separate calendar just for habits and add an event after each one.

What it does well: the month view gives you a visual record, and it costs nothing.

Where it falls short: Calendar is built for appointments, so logging takes several taps, it cannot count anything, and your habit entries clutter your real schedule unless you hide that calendar.

3. A Notes checklist or a paper tracker

In the Notes app you can make a checklist with one line per day, or a table with your habits across the top and dates down the side. Paper works the same way: a grid on the fridge, filled in with a pen.

What it does well: it is completely flexible, private, and satisfying to fill in. Paper in particular is hard to ignore when it is on the wall. If you like this route, our printable habit tracker gives you a ready-made monthly grid.

Where it falls short: nothing reminds you, nothing adds up your streak, and a note does not tell you that you did best on Tuesdays. You also have to start a fresh sheet every month.

What the built-in options miss

Built-in apps are fine for a quick start. In practice, people who stick with tracking usually want a few things that none of them offer together:

  • A visible streak and a best streak, so a run of good days feels worth protecting.
  • Number goals, such as 8 glasses of water, 20 pages or 30 minutes, not just done or not done.
  • Limits for habits you want less of, such as under 2 hours of screen time.
  • Weekly targets, such as the gym three times a week, without breaking a daily streak on rest days.
  • A history at a glance, so you can see a whole year of effort in one picture.
  • Quick logging from a widget or Siri, because every extra tap is a reason to skip.

How to start tracking without giving up

  1. Start with one to three habits. More than that in week one is the most common reason people stop.
  2. Make each habit specific. "Read 10 pages after dinner" beats "read more".
  3. Put the tracker where you will see it. A Home Screen or Lock Screen widget turns a glance at your phone into a reminder. See how to add a habit tracker widget.
  4. Expect it to take a while. Research suggests habits take weeks to months to feel automatic, not 21 days. Our guide on how long it takes to form a habit explains the numbers.
  5. Do not quit after a miss. One missed day does little harm; read what to do when you miss a day.

FAQ

Does iPhone have a built-in habit tracker?

No, iPhone has no dedicated habit tracker, but a repeating reminder in the Reminders app works as a simple one because you tick it off and it returns the next day.

Can I track habits in Apple Health?

Apple Health records health data such as steps, sleep and water, but it does not track general habits like reading or journaling with streaks.

Is a habit tracker app better than paper?

Paper is flexible and hard to ignore on a wall, while an app adds reminders, automatic streaks, number goals and a long history, so the better choice depends on which of those you need.

Do I need an account to track habits on iPhone?

Not always; Reminders, Notes and some habit trackers, including Habit Forever, work without creating an account.

How many habits should I track at once?

Starting with one to three habits makes it much easier to keep going, and you can add more once those feel routine.
